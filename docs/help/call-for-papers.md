A community's owners and admins can open a call for papers: a page where speakers send proposals for talks, with the answers and files you ask for. The same call works for sponsors or anything else people apply for — its title says what it is.

A call is for one of the community's events, or for the whole community, as a standing "propose a talk" form.

---

### Creating a call

1. On the community's manage page, open the **CFP** tab and select **New call for papers**.
2. Give it a title and pick its **Event** — one of the community's upcoming events — or **No event — a call for the whole community**. An event has one call for papers.
3. Select **Create**. The call opens on its own page as a **Draft**, which only the community's owners and admins see.

Under **Settings**:

- **Description** — the call itself: topics, formats, how you choose. It supports [markdown](/help/markdown).
- **Opens** and **Closes** — when proposals are taken. Picking an event sets **Closes** to the event's start; change it as you need. A call with no closing date stays open until you close it.

---

### Questions

Every proposal has a title and a description, and comes with the speaker's name, picture and email. Under **Questions**, add anything else you need:

- **File upload** — one file per question; to ask for slides and a paper, add two. Speakers can upload PDF, PowerPoint, Keynote, Word, Excel and images (JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC), up to 100 MB per file.
- **Short text** with **Long answer** turned on — a few paragraphs, for a bio or notes.
- **Choice** — talk length, track, or sponsorship tier.

The other types are the ones [registration questions](/help/guest-questions) use. Mark a question **Required** when every proposal needs it.

---

### Opening and closing

Select **Open for proposals** to open the call now, or set an **Opens** date to open it later. The call closes on its **Closes** date, or when you select **Close now**. **Open for proposals** on a closed call opens it again, with no closing date.

While a call is open:

- **For an event** — the event's page has a **Call for papers** section after the speakers, with the closing time and **Submit a proposal**. A **Call for papers** chip at the top of the page leads to it, and the event's card shows **CFP open** in event lists.
- **For the whole community** — the community's page shows it under the community's name, with **Submit a proposal**.

Every call has its own page — the link to share. **View** on the call's manage page opens it. A call for an event is seen only by people who can see the event. Once the call closes, its page says so.

---

### Sending a proposal

1. Open the call's page — from the event, the community's page, or a link the organizers shared. Signed out, **Submit a proposal** asks you to sign in first.
2. The form shows your name, picture and email: they go to the organizers with your proposal.
3. Fill in the title, the description and the organizers' questions. The description supports [markdown](/help/markdown), without images. Select **Send**.

You can send more than one proposal to the same call. They are listed under **Your proposals** on the call's page, and on the **CFP** tab of your events, with their status: **Submitted**, **Accepted**, **Declined** or **Withdrawn**. You are notified when the organizers accept or decline one.

- **Edit** — while the call is open and the organizers have not decided. They see when you last edited it.
- **Withdraw** — any time. The organizers keep its title; the description, answers and files are deleted. If it had been accepted, they are notified.

---

### Reviewing proposals

The community's owners and admins are notified of every new proposal. The **CFP** tab's badge counts the proposals waiting for a decision, across all the community's calls.

On the call's page, **Proposals** lists them, waiting ones first. Filter them by **Status**, or select **Export** for a CSV: name, username, email, title, description, status, dates, and one column per question.

Select a proposal to read it: the speaker and their email, the description, the answers, and the files, each with **Download**. Files have no public link — only the speaker and the community's owners and admins can download them.

At the bottom, pick **Accepted** or **Declined**. The speaker is notified and can no longer edit the proposal. To undo a decision, pick **Submitted**.

---

### The next event's call

**Duplicate** on a call's page makes a new draft with the same title, description and questions — without dates, an event or proposals. Pick the next event and open it.

---

### If the event changes

When an event moves out of the community, it leaves its call for papers: an open call closes, a scheduled one goes back to a draft, and the proposals stay with the community that ran it. The event's new community can open a call of its own. A call for a cancelled event stops showing.

---

### Deleting a call

**Delete**, in the **Danger zone** at the bottom of a call's page, deletes the call and every proposal sent to it.
